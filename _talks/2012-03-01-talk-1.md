Family homelessness remains an urgent social issue in Canada, yet children’s experiences of homelessness continue to be underrepresented in homelessness research and public discourse. Drawing on findings from an activity-based research project conducted in partnership with a community organization serving families experiencing homelessness in Southern Ontario, we reflected on how children engaged in creative, performance-based activities to create friendships and support networks, navigate tensions and conflicts, and actively contribute to community-building during periods of uncertainty.

Specifically, we shared both child-initiated activities, including TikTok dance challenges and Musical Chairs, and researcher-initiated activities, including Pick and Act, Puppet Theatre, the Yarn Activity, and Come All My Friends. Across these activities, children demonstrated agency, creativity, and capacities for relationship-building, collaboration, conflict resolution, and community-making. Our findings challenged deficit-oriented understandings of children experiencing homelessness and highlighted performance-based methods as valuable spaces for connection, belonging, and mutual support amid housing instability.
